# 08 · Welfare information request

Sample `SYN-0010`, printed in small type at the bottom right of the page. One page. A letter dated 12 October 2026 from a fictional welfare agency, the Public Payments Office, to Mrs M A Wilson. The agency could not match the details of the pension she draws from her superannuation fund, so it asks her for the balance of that account. The answer key is [`ground-truth.json`](ground-truth.json).

## This version is a redo

The earlier version of this letter, dated 14 July 2026, mixed two different kinds of letter. Its first paragraph called it "a routine compliance check". A compliance program letter asks the person to phone the agency within 28 days. The rest of the letter followed a different kind: the income stream review letter, which gives 21 days. The earlier letter also printed a period under review, asked for three figures, and listed an enclosed form, a reply paid envelope, and ways to send the form by post or in person. The agency's page asks for none of that with this kind of pension. Its legal sentences also came from three different kinds of notice under the Act.

This version follows the income stream review letter throughout. These things changed: the date of the letter; the first paragraph; the period row in the table, now removed; what she is asked to give; the ways she can give it (now three, with a new web address for the access code and the reply paid address removed); the enclosure line, now removed; the paragraph on what happens if she does not reply, now limited to one kind of notice; and the paragraph on disagreeing with a decision. The layout, the names, the reference numbers, the access code and the phone numbers did not change. The official facts were checked on 16 September 2026 and are recorded, with quotes, in [`source-checks.md`](source-checks.md) beside this file.

## Where it comes from

This letter did not follow an official sample letter. No real letter of this kind was available to copy. The federal agency that sends these letters keeps its letter templates on an internal website that the public cannot open.

So the letter was built up from what the Social Security (Administration) Act 1999 says a notice like this must contain, from the Australian Government's guidance on writing letters, and from the agency's public web pages about this kind of review. The look of the page follows the layout habits of one official document from the same agency. That document is a blank form, not a letter.

- **Income Stream Reviews** (Services Australia, Age Pension page). The page about this kind of review, and the source of the story. Twice a year, in August and February, the agency gets the details of account-based income streams from the funds. If a fund does not give the information, or the agency cannot match it, the agency writes "in early October or early April". The person must give the details "within 21 days from the date on your letter". For an account-based income stream that started on or after 1 January 2015, the person only needs to give "the most recent account balance on 1 July or later", and "You don't have to provide us with a new income stream schedule". The details are given online, through the person's online account or with a One Time Access Code sent in the letter together with the Customer Reference Number, or by phone if the person cannot do it online. These facts gave the first paragraph, the one balance asked for under "What you need to do", the sentence about not sending a schedule, the 21 days, and the three ways under "How to give us the details". Its sentences were rewritten, not copied. <https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/income-stream-reviews?context=22526>
- **Income and assets form (MOD IA), June 2026 edition** (Services Australia). A blank official form of 20 pages. It is the only official document whose look was followed. It gave the layout: headings in a left column and text in a right column, a thin rule across the page under the letterhead, the agency name with a black band under it holding one italic lower case word, web addresses and phone numbers in bold with no underline, a small form code with a year and month version number at the bottom left, and the page number in bold at the bottom. It also gave the label "Customer Reference Number" and the three footer headings "Information in your language", "Hearing and speech assistance" and "Privacy and your personal information". The text under those footer headings was rewritten. <https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/mod-ia>
- **Australian Public Service Writing Handbook** (Australian Public Service Commission, November 2024). It gave the order of the letter: why we are writing, what you need to do, when you need to do it by, what happens if you do not, how to give us the details, what to do if you disagree, and a closing sentence. It also gave "Dear Mrs Wilson" and "Yours sincerely". It gave the rule that a printed letter spells out the full web address. It gave the way dates are written, such as "12 October 2026". <https://www.apsc.gov.au/publication/australian-public-service-writing-handbook>
- **Style Manual, Emails and letters** (Australian Government). It gave the recipient's name and address at the top, the date of the letter and a subject line that says what the letter is for. Nothing is enclosed with this letter, so it has no list of enclosures. <https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/content-types/emails-and-letters>
- **Social Security (Administration) Act 1999, section 196** (Parliament of Australia, text from AustLII). A written notice asking for information must describe the information, say how to give it, and say how long the person has. It must also say that it is given under this section. That is the bold sentence on the page, "This notice is given under section 196 of the Social Security (Administration) Act 1999." The sentence was written from the words of the section, not copied from a real letter. No real income stream review letter was found, so whether real letters cite the section by number is not known. Section 196(3) says the time given must not end earlier than 14 days after the notice is given, unless the Secretary is satisfied that a shorter time is reasonable. <https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ssa1999338/s196.html>
- **Social Security (Administration) Act 1999, section 81** (Parliament of Australia, text from AustLII, read 16 September 2026). If a person receiving a payment is given "a notice embodying a requirement under Division 1 of Part 5" and does not comply, the Secretary may cancel or suspend the payment. Section 196 is in that Division. This gave the sentence "If these details do not reach us in time, your pension can be suspended or cancelled." The Income Stream Reviews page does not say in its own words what happens when the 21 days pass for this kind of income stream, so the sentence follows the Act. The earlier version also said she could stop qualifying for her concession card, and that the letter was "an information notice". Those come from sections 64 and 72, which apply to other kinds of notice (section 63 notices, and notices under sections 67 to 70), so this version leaves them out. <https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ssa1999338/s81.html>
- **Social Security Guide 8.1.3.10, Provision of information** (Department of Social Services). It confirms that a notice under this part of the Act must give the person at least 14 days to reply (the guide does not mention the exception in section 196(3)). The 21 days in this letter meet that minimum. <https://guides.dss.gov.au/social-security-guide/8/1/3/10>
- **Change of circumstances (Age Pension)** (Services Australia). It gave the ongoing duty to report a change, in the paragraph under "What else you need to tell us". <https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/change-circumstances-when-you-get-age-pension?context=22526>
- **Explanations and formal reviews of a Centrelink decision** (Services Australia). It gave the paragraph under "If you disagree with a decision". A formal review is done by an Authorised Review Officer, whom the page calls "an independent officer"; the letter says "an independent review officer". A person can ask for an explanation "at any time". A person "should apply for a formal review of most claim and entitlement decisions within 13 weeks", can still apply after 13 weeks, and if the decision changes may only get the entitlement from the date of applying. A formal review costs nothing. <https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/explanations-and-formal-reviews-centrelink-decision?context=64107>
- **Centrepay Procedural Guide, version 6, March 2024** (Services Australia). Only one fact was taken from it. Section 4.1.2 says the agency gives a Customer Reference Number to businesses as well as to customers. Both kinds of number have the same format. The letter uses this fact when it gives Wattlebank Super a reference number in the same format as Mrs Wilson's. The real format described in the guide, 9 numbers and 1 letter, was not used. <https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/centrepay>

Made up, not taken from any source: the agency name Public Payments Office, the word "paystead" in the black band, the superannuation fund Wattlebank Super, Mrs Wilson with her address and date of birth, the officer Karen Mitchell, the start date of her pension, the two reference numbers themselves and their pattern of PPO followed by two groups of four digits, the one-time access code, every phone number, the office hours, the web addresses, and the form code RVW452.2607. The date of the letter was chosen: the source says "early October", and 12 October was taken so that the 21 days end in November. Every sentence of the letter was written for this sample, because no real letter wording could be found. Printing a Public Payments Office number for the fund on the letter, under the label "Provider reference", is also invented, with no source for it. The sentence "If the account has closed, tell us the closing date instead." has no source. Some layout choices differ from the official form on purpose. The font is Arial at 11 point, where the form uses a narrow font. There is no government crest and no national government line above the agency name. Only one phone number is given for hearing and speech help, where the form gives two.

## The six fields

### document_type

**Welfare information request notice.** Page 1, the bold heading under the address reads "We need some details about your income stream". The first paragraph says "This time we could not match the information from Wattlebank Super to your income stream, so we need you to give it to us yourself. This is a routine review, not a change to your pension." Under "What happens if you do not", the letter says "This notice is given under section 196 of the Social Security (Administration) Act 1999." The answer key stores it as `welfare_information_request_notice`. This field is free text and the experiments do not score it.

### issuer

**Public Payments Office.** Page 1, top left, the letterhead reads "Public Payments Office" in large bold type, with a black band under it. The same name is printed as the last line of the signature block, under "Karen Mitchell" and "Service Officer". The letter is written and signed in the name of this office. It is also the office she has to give the details to.

### action_required

**Contact Public Payments Office.** Page 1, the section "What you need to do" says "Tell us the balance of the income stream above on 1 July 2026." The section "When you need to do it by" gives her 21 days. The section "How to give us the details" lists three ways: online through her account, online with the one-time access code, and by telephone if she cannot do it online. The section "What happens if you do not" says her pension can be suspended or cancelled if the details do not reach the office in time. No paper form comes with the letter. What she has to do is get one figure to the office before a deadline, and for a woman of 78 the most practical way is to phone them before 2 November. Of the contract's eight action words, Contact is the one for that. The value starts with one of the contract's eight action words, and the experiments score that word.

The key also accepts **Return form to Public Payments Office** (`also_accepted` in the answer key). The letter asks her to give the office information it requested, and Return form is the contract's word for handing information back, so a reader that gives it has not misread the page either. This was the first answer until 24 September 2026; it moved second because every reading in experiments 09 on chose Contact (KAN-68). Either word must come with the due date: the date is what she most needs from this letter.

### due_date

**2026-11-02.** This date is not printed on the letter. Page 1, the section "When you need to do it by" says "You have 21 days from the date at the top of this notice to give us these details." The date at the top right of the page, beside the label "Date", is "12 October 2026". Counting 21 days from 12 October 2026 gives 2 November 2026. The first 19 days reach 31 October, and 2 more days reach 2 November. The answer key writes it as year, month, day. The other dates on the page are not due dates: "1 July 2026" is the day the balance is taken on, "1 September 2019" is when her pension started, and "14 March 1948" is her date of birth.

### amount

**No payment required.** No dollar amount and no currency symbol is printed anywhere on the page. The letter asks for information. It does not ask for money. The balance she is asked for is a figure she gives to the office, and it is not printed on the letter. The three ways listed under "How to give us the details" are ways to give the details, and none of them is a way to pay.

### reference

**PPO-5002-9891.** Page 1, top right, beside the label "Customer Reference Number". The number itself is printed only once. Under "How to give us the details", way 2 is "Online with a code". It tells her to "enter your Customer Reference Number together with the one-time access code below". The contract asks for the reference, account or customer number the person must quote, and this is the number the letter tells her to enter.

### identifiers

Every number the letter prints that identifies her, something she holds, or this matter, each under the label printed beside it, written as printed. The first list is the numbers a reading must find; the reference is one of them, and a reading that gives any of them as the reference is right. The experiments score whether every number in the first list is found.

- **Customer Reference Number PPO-5002-9891.** page 1, top right header block, second line, bold beside the grey label.

Also printed, and identifying something narrower: a payment method's own reference, a meter, an invoice or statement, a provider, or a one-time code. A reading may list these or leave them out.

- **Provider reference PPO-7143-4935.** page 1, "Income stream under review" table, fourth row.
- **One-time access code VRJF86.** page 1, boxed panel directly under the three numbered ways, printed in a serif typewriter face with wide letter spacing as V R J F 8 6.
