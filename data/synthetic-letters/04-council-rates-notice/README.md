# 04 · Council rates notice

Sample `SYN-0004`, printed in small type at the bottom right of each page. Two pages. A yearly valuation and rates notice from a fictional council, Calderfield City Council, to Mrs M A Wilson, for the year 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027. The answer key is [`ground-truth.json`](ground-truth.json).

## Where it comes from

Page 1 of this notice follows an official annotated example. Manningham Council, a council in Victoria, publishes a sample 2026 to 27 Valuation and Rates Notice on its website, with eleven numbered notes that explain each part. The sample uses a made-up ratepayer and made-up numbers. Page 1 copies its blocks, their order and their labels. The sample shows only the front of the notice, so page 2 had no example to follow. Page 2 was built up from the list of contents that Victorian regulations require on a rates notice. No real person's rates notice was used.

Only Manningham's layout was followed. Councils differ in which charges and payment options they offer, and a notice that mixed several councils' layouts would contradict itself. Pages from other councils were used for numbers and for checks, not for layout. Like the Manningham sample, this notice has no municipal charge and no option to pay the whole year in one payment.

- **How to read your valuation and rates notice** (Manningham Council). The sample notice on this page is the only official picture of a rates notice that was used. It gave the layout of page 1, block by block: the contact lines at the top, the logo at top right, the ABN line above the title, "TAX INVOICE", "Date of issue" and "Property ID" at top right, the grey box with the amount and date of the first instalment, the eRates box, the "Property details" and "Property valuation" boxes side by side, the two charge boxes side by side with a total each, the TOTAL, the interest line, the four columns of "HOW TO PAY" with the 4 and 10 instalment options, the interpreter line and the tear-off slip. It also gave the line labels in the charge boxes, the EPA Landfill Levy amount of \$72.00, and the way a rebate is printed with the minus sign before the dollar sign. <https://www.manningham.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-07/valuation_rates_notice_example_2026_0.jpg>
- **Local Government (General) Regulations 2025, regulation 6** (Victorian legislation). The list of what a rates notice must contain. This list decided the sections of page 2: the rates cap, the differential rates table, penalties for late payment, the payment plan, the order in which payments are allocated, and the rights to review and object. The table must show every class of land, the rate for each class, and what this property would pay in each class. The regulation also gave the exact wording of the rates cap statement at the top of page 2. <https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/in-force/statutory-rules/local-government-general-regulations-2025>
- **Local Government Act 1989** (Victorian legislation). The rule that a council must let ratepayers pay in four instalments. Section 172 on interest for late payment, which the "Penalties for late payment" paragraph on page 2 cites. Sections 183, 184 and 185, which the "Other review rights" paragraph cites. <https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/in-force/acts/local-government-act-1989>
- **Emergency Services and Volunteers Fund Act 2012** (Victorian legislation). What the notice must show about this State levy: a fixed charge, and a variable charge worked out as the levy rate times the capital improved value, both printed on the rates notice itself. The \$50.00 pension rebate on the levy, which is set in the Act. The change of name from Fire Services Property Levy on 1 July 2025, which the note under the charge boxes explains. <https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/in-force/acts/fire-services-property-levy-act-2012>
- **Emergency services and volunteers fund current rates** (State Revenue Office Victoria). The 2026 to 27 residential fixed charge of \$139.00 and the residential rate of 17.3 cents per \$1,000 of capital improved value. <https://www.sro.vic.gov.au/about-us/rates-and-statistics/current-rates/emergency-services-and-volunteers-fund-current-rates>
- **Pensioner concession for rates** (Bayside City Council). The 2026 to 27 limit of \$272.70 on the pension rebate for council rates. <https://www.bayside.vic.gov.au/apply-pay-and-report/about-your-rates/pensioner-concession-rates>
- **Understand your rates and waste charge** (Merri-bek City Council). The residential rate in the dollar, 0.00233951, used for the General Rate line. <https://www.merri-bek.vic.gov.au/living-in-merri-bek/rates/understand-your-rates/>
- **Your rates notice** (City of Port Phillip). The 2026 to 27 waste charge of \$278.00, used for the Waste Standard Service line. <https://www.portphillip.vic.gov.au/council-services/rates-and-valuations/your-rates-notice>
- **Annual council rate caps** (Essential Services Commission). The 2026 to 27 rates cap of 2.75 per cent, printed in the "Rates cap" section on page 2. <https://www.esc.vic.gov.au/local-government/annual-council-rate-caps>
- **Valuation Best Practice Specifications Guidelines 2026** (Valuer-General Victoria). The property classification code 110 and its name, Detached Dwelling, printed in the "Property details" box. <https://www.land.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0027/752814/valuation-best-practice-specifications-guidelines-2026.pdf>
- **Valuation of Land Act 1960** (Victorian legislation). The law that page 2 names for valuations and for objections to them. <https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/in-force/acts/valuation-land-act-1960>
- **Objection form, residential** (Valuer-General Victoria). The rules in "Objecting to your valuation" on page 2: an objection must be in writing, must state its grounds, must be lodged within two months, and goes to the council that sent the notice. <https://www.land.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0025/551860/objection-form-residential.pdf>
- **Municipal Rates Concession application form, September 2025** (Department of Families, Fairness and Housing, Victoria). How the pension concession on council rates works. The conditions in the last sentence of the "Concessions" paragraph on page 2: the property must be the main home, used only as a home, and only one property can be claimed each rating year. <https://services.dffh.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-09/Municipal%20Rates%20Concession%20-%20application%20form%20-%20%20September%202025.pdf>
- **Ministerial Guidelines Relating to Payment of Rates and Charges** (Victoria Government Gazette, No. S 747, 24 December 2025). The basis for the "Payment difficulties" paragraph on page 2: contact the council, ask for a payment plan, and in some cases ask for a deferral or waiver. <https://www.gazette.vic.gov.au/gazette/Gazettes2025/GG2025S747.pdf>
- **Understanding your rates notice 2025** (Monash City Council). Used as a check only. Its layout was not used, because it is an explainer graphic and not a notice. It confirmed that the general rate, the waste charge and the Emergency Services and Volunteers Fund levy together make up the rates bill. <https://www.monash.vic.gov.au/files/assets/public/v/4/residents-property/documents/understanding-your-rates-notice-2025.pdf>
- **Privacy and Data Protection Act 2014** (Victorian legislation). The law named in the "Privacy" paragraph on page 2.

Made up, not taken from any source: the council name Calderfield City Council, the post outlet name Kelsworth Post, the ABN, the PO Box, every phone number, email address and web address, the Property ID, the biller and payment codes, every reference number, the ratepayer Mrs M A Wilson, and her address. The land title numbers are invented. Only their format of lot, plan, volume and folio follows the Manningham sample. The capital improved value of \$782,000 and the site value of \$495,000 are invented. The rates for the four other classes of land in the page 2 table are invented, and were set as fixed multiples of the residential rate. The layout of page 2 is invented: the order of its sections, their headings and their look. The page 2 table does not mark which row is her class of land. That was a choice made without any sample of the table to follow. The waste charge is printed as one line, which puts Port Phillip's amount into Manningham's layout. The rule for rounding the instalments was worked out from the two instalment plans on the Manningham sample, and was not found written down anywhere. Page 2 says "the State valuation authority", "the State revenue authority" and "the relevant tribunal" in place of real agency names. The banking and post boxes carry no payment network brand names. The biller and payment codes have seven digits, while the codes on the Manningham sample have four. The QR code and the barcode are patterns only and cannot be scanned. The interpreter number is printed with its (03) area code. The colours and the logo are invented.

## The six fields

### document_type

**Council rates notice.** Page 1, top left, the title reads "Valuation and Rates Notice", and under it "For the period of 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027". Page 1, top right, says "TAX INVOICE". Page 2 opens with the same title, followed by "further information". The answer key stores it as `council_rates_notice`. This field is free text and the experiments do not score it.

### issuer

**Calderfield City Council.** Page 1, top right, the logo reads "CALDERFIELD" and under it "CITY COUNCIL". The full name is printed at the top left, just above the title: "Calderfield City Council ABN 77 422 627 311". It is printed again at the top of the tear-off slip, just below the dashed cut line ("make it payable to Calderfield City Council"), and at the start of the "Privacy" paragraph on page 2. It is the council that sends the notice, and payments are made payable to it.

### action_required

**Pay the first instalment of the 2026 to 27 rates and charges.** Page 1, top right, the grey box with "Amount Due:" and "Due Date:", and the "HOW TO PAY" section across the lower part of the page. The tear-off slip at the bottom of page 1 says "FIRST INSTALMENT \$535.09". The notice sets out the rates and charges for 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027 and shows how to pay them. The amount and date in the grey box are the first row of the "Option A" instalments, and the slip names that payment as the first instalment. This field is free text and the experiments do not score it.

### due_date

**2026-09-30.** Page 1, top right, the grey box, "Due Date: 30 September 2026". The same date is the first row of the "Option A" instalments in "HOW TO PAY", written "30/09/2026". The grey box gives it as the due date for the amount due, and the Option A table shows it is the date of the first instalment. The page writes it day first, the Australian way. The answer key writes it as year, month, day.

### amount

**\$535.09.** Page 1, top right, the grey box, "Amount Due: \$535.09". The same amount is the first row of the "Option A" instalments in "HOW TO PAY", the grey "Amount due" box at the bottom of that column, and "FIRST INSTALMENT \$535.09" on the tear-off slip. It is the first of the four instalments under "Option A". Those four instalments, \$535.09 and three of \$532.00, add up to the TOTAL of \$2,131.09 printed above "HOW TO PAY".

### reference

**79475.** Page 1, top right, under the date of issue, "Property ID: 79475". The same number is printed at the bottom left of the tear-off slip under "Property ID", and at the top of page 2 ("Property ID: 79475"). The contract asks for the reference, account or customer number the person must quote. The Property ID is the number this notice identifies her property by, and it is printed on the slip she attaches to a cheque.
