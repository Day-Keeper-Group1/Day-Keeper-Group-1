# 18 · Medicare benefit statement

Sample `SYN-0011`, printed in small type at the bottom right of each page. Two pages. A statement from a fictional public health scheme, the Patient Rebate Scheme, to Mrs M A Wilson, listing seven medical services from June and July 2026 and the benefits the scheme has already paid for them. The answer key is [`ground-truth.json`](ground-truth.json).

## Where it comes from

This statement did not follow an official sample or an annotated example document. It was built up from published guidance and official data, without one.

The agency that issues this kind of statement publishes its own operational manual, and that manual lists the fields a statement carries. The customer facing template itself was never obtained: the manual's resources page links to an internal site, and the public copy of that page returns nothing. So the field list and the payment method wording are official and word for word, while the English used in the column headings and the arrangement of the page were worked out from the manual's description. The rules the synthetic data was made under do not allow a real person's statement to be used as a template.

- **Operational Blueprint 133-10010000, Medicare claims history and claims history statement online** (Services Australia). It lists, in order, the seven things a statement shows for each service: the date of service, the item number with a short description, the total cost of the service, the benefit paid, the cost to the claimant, the payment method, and a reason code. That is the order of the columns in the table on page 1. <https://operational.servicesaustralia.gov.au/public/Pages/self-service/133-10010000-01.html>
- **Operational Blueprint 011-44150000, Medicare cards** (Services Australia). It gave the shape of the identity block at the top right of both pages: a ten digit card number, an individual reference number between 1 and 9, and a card expiry written as a month and a year. <https://operational.servicesaustralia.gov.au/public/Pages/your-health/011-44150000-01.html>
- **Operational Blueprint 011-43080110, Medicare Safety Net letters** (Services Australia). It describes a separate family of safety net letters, which are written as paragraphs rather than as a table. It was used to keep this statement out of that family: the safety net box on page 1 reports what has been counted this calendar year and never says she has reached the threshold. <https://operational.servicesaustralia.gov.au/public/Pages/your-health/011-43080110-01.html>
- **Understand your Medicare statements** (Services Australia). It gave the five payment method values and the official wording of what each one means, which is the first table on page 2. It also gave the notes under "Other things worth knowing" about which provider name is shown for pathology and for optometry. <https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/understand-your-medicare-statements?context=60092>
- **Medicare claims** (Services Australia). It gave the title "Statement of claim and benefit", the four ways a claim can be made, which is the "How you claimed" line in the "About this claim" box, and the official processing time of 21 days for a claim sent by mail. The claim was received on 28/07/2026 and the statement is dated 11 August 2026, fourteen days later. <https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/medicare-claims?context=60092>
- **Medicare Safety Nets** (Services Australia). It gave the way the safety net counts: over a calendar year, and only payments the person has already made in full. Both of those are printed in the box on page 1. <https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/medicare-safety-nets>
- **What are the Medicare Safety Nets thresholds** (Services Australia). It gave the tier name "Concessional and Family Tax Benefit Part A" and the threshold amount \$861.20 on page 1. <https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/what-are-medicare-safety-nets-thresholds?context=22001>
- **How Medicare Safety Nets and thresholds work** (Services Australia). It says a benefit is 85 to 100 percent of the schedule fee, which is the rate behind every figure in the "Benefit paid" column. <https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/how-medicare-safety-nets-and-thresholds-work?context=22001>
- **Write to us** (Services Australia). It gave the form of the postal address on page 2, which names no city and reads "In your capital city". <https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/write-to-us?context=64107>
- **Medicare Benefits Schedule data file, 1 March 2026** (MBS Online, Australian Government Department of Health, Disability and Ageing). It gave the benefit figures for the five services dated in June 2026. <https://www.mbsonline.gov.au/internet/mbsonline/publishing.nsf/Content/Downloads-20260301>
- **Medicare Benefits Schedule data file, 1 July 2026** (MBS Online, Australian Government Department of Health, Disability and Ageing). It gave the benefit figures for the two services dated 21/07/2026. The schedule changed on 1 July 2026, so item 00023 pays \$43.90 in June and \$45.05 in July, while item 73805 pays \$3.90 on both dates. <https://www.mbsonline.gov.au/internet/mbsonline/publishing.nsf/Content/Downloads-20260701>
- **Basic Service Description for Assignment of Medicare Benefits** (published with the 1 July 2026 Medicare Benefits Schedule files). It is a list of item numbers with their official short descriptions, and it gave the wording of the description on each row of the table. <https://www.mbsonline.gov.au/internet/mbsonline/publishing.nsf/Content/Downloads-20260701>
- **MOD IA income and assets form, June 2026** (Services Australia). A blank official form, used only for the page furniture this agency prints on everything: the black bar with a word in white italic lowercase, the rule under the header, and the footer with a form code and a page number. The body of this statement is a table and looks nothing like that form. <https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/mod-ia>
- **Health Insurance Act 1973** (Commonwealth, Federal Register of Legislation). It is the law that sets up the benefits and the item numbers behind the "Benefit paid" column. The letter does not quote it. <https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00101/latest/text>

Made up, not taken from any source: the scheme name Patient Rebate Scheme and the word claimstead in the black bar, which stand in for the name and the brand of the real public health scheme; the recipient Mrs Margaret Anne Wilson with her address and date of birth; the four providers Mr Andrew Reid, Dr Susan Patel, Dr John Anderson and Ms Emma Brown; and every number that identifies somebody: the rebate card number, the individual reference number, the four provider numbers, the statement number, the claim number, the phone numbers, the post office box number, the web address and the form code at the foot of the page. The English wording of the column headings, the order of the columns across the page, and the fact that the explanations are printed on the back were worked out from the operational manual, because no customer facing template was obtained. The seven figures in the "Charged" column are invented: the schedule fixes only the benefit, and what a provider actually charges is a market matter that cannot be looked up, so each charge was chosen inside the price range the spec card gives for that kind of visit. The "Cost to claimant" column follows by subtraction. On the optometry row the provider took the benefit as full payment, so the charge and the benefit are both \$66.15 and the cost to her is \$0.00. The \$458.30 counted so far in the safety net box is a set value, chosen below the \$861.20 threshold. The card expiry 08/2028 is written the official way and its value is invented. The code BB and its explanation on page 2 are ours: no official table of codes was found, so one neutral two letter code was used and defined on the back. Putting seven services from two months into one claim sent by mail is also a choice, because nothing official says how many services one claim may hold. Page 2 explains all five payment methods the scheme uses, while this statement uses only two of them, because the back of the form is the same on every statement. The national coat of arms and the words Australian Government are left off the header, and the benefit column is printed as "Benefit paid" rather than the longer official column name that also says who paid it, because a made up scheme must not present itself as a real government body.

## The six fields

### document_type

**Medicare benefit statement.** Page 1, below the address block, the large heading "Statement of claim and benefit", and under it "For services from 1 June 2026 to 31 July 2026". The table below lists seven services with what was charged, what benefit was paid, and what each service cost her. Page 2 is headed "How to read this statement" and explains the payment methods and codes used on page 1. The answer key stores it as `medicare_benefit_statement`. This field is free text and the experiments do not score it.

### issuer

**Patient Rebate Scheme.** Page 1, top left, the first line of the letterhead in large serif type above a rule, "Patient Rebate Scheme". The same letterhead is printed at the top left of page 2. The letter's own prose uses the scheme's name too: page 1 has the section heading "Patient Rebate Scheme Safety Net", and the last line of page 2 gives the web address "patient-rebate-scheme.example". Below the name, a black bar carries the word "claimstead" in white italic lowercase. The scheme is the body that received the claim, paid the benefits and sent this statement.

### action_required

**No action.** Page 1, the last line on the page, below the "About this claim" box: "The benefits shown above have been paid. Nothing further is needed from you for these services." The "Payment method" column on every row says where the benefit has already gone, either "EFT to Claimant" or "Bulk Bill". Page 2 has a heading "If something does not look right" with contact lines, and those apply only if she thinks something on the statement is wrong. The value starts with one of the contract's eight action words, and the experiments score that word.

### due_date

**Not applicable.** No due date, pay by date or reply by date is printed on either page. Page 1 ends with "The benefits shown above have been paid. Nothing further is needed from you for these services." The statement reports services that have already happened and benefits that have already been paid, so there is nothing she has to do by a date. The answer key has `null` for this field.

### amount

**No payment required.** Page 1, the last line on the page, says "The benefits shown above have been paid. Nothing further is needed from you for these services." The three money columns in the table are records of money that has already moved: "Charged" is what each provider charged, "Benefit paid" is what the scheme paid back, and "Cost to claimant" is what the service ended up costing her after the benefit. The totals row reads "Totals \$598.15 \$292.40 \$305.75". There is no amount due, no payment slip and no way to pay printed anywhere on either page. The answer key has `null` for this field.

### reference

**4378 31830 2.** Page 1, top right, the first line of the identity block, "Rebate card number" with "4378 31830 2" beside it. The same block is printed at the top right of page 2. The contract asks for the reference, account or customer number the person must quote. The card number is the number the scheme holds her records under, and it is printed in the header of both pages, across from the scheme's own name.
