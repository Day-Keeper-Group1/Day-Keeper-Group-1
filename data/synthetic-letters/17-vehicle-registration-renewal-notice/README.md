# 17 · Vehicle registration renewal notice

Sample `SYN-0009`, printed in small type at the bottom right of each page. Two pages. A notice from a fictional government registry, Driver and Vehicle Registry, to Mrs M A Wilson, asking her to pay to renew the registration of her car, with her certificate of registration printed on page 2. The answer key is [`ground-truth.json`](ground-truth.json).

## Where it comes from

Half of this notice followed an official sample document and half of it did not. The Victorian road authority publishes a sample picture of a renewal notice, filled in with made-up example details, on its own certificate of registration page. In that picture the certificate is readable, and the half that shows the renewal notice itself is deliberately blurred out. So nine of the ten field labels in the green certificate box on page 2 come from that official picture. The tenth, the expiry date, is not part of that picture. The whole of page 1 was built up from the regulations and the published fee tables, with no sample to look at.

- **Road Safety (Vehicles) Regulations 2021, version 011** (Victorian Legislation). Regulation 81 says the one thing a renewal notice must contain: a date, and the fact that the registration expires if it is not renewed by that date. It also says that not receiving a notice does not excuse the owner, which is the second paragraph of "Important information" on page 2. Regulation 56 lists the eight things a certificate of registration must show. It also lets the registrar add other information it considers appropriate. Between the two, they cover the labels in the green box on page 2. Regulation 58 allows a light vehicle to be registered for 3, 6 or 12 months, which is the "Other registration periods" table. Regulation 82 says a renewal is the registration fee plus the transport accident charge, which is why one notice collects money for two bodies. <https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/in-force/statutory-rules/road-safety-vehicles-regulations-2021/011>
- **Transport accident charge premium rates, from 1 July 2026** (Transport Accident Commission). The pensioner page of this rate table gives three of the figures in the charges table exactly as printed: the transport accident charge of \$275.50, the \$25.05 of GST inside it, and the duty of \$27.55. The same table says a 6 month term costs half the year and a 3 month term costs a quarter, and it lists the postcodes that put a car in the high risk zone. <https://www.tac.vic.gov.au/about-the-tac/our-organisation/transport-accident-charge/transport-accident-charge-premium-rates>
- **Vehicle fees** (VicRoads). The published yearly total for a passenger car, which the registration fee was worked out from, and the \$2.70 short term service fee shown on the 3 month and 6 month rows. <https://www.vicroads.vic.gov.au/registrations/fees-and-charges/vehicle-fees>
- **Concession holder discounts** (VicRoads). A pensioner concession card takes half off the registration fee, and it must be applied for at least five days before the registration is due. Both facts are on page 1, in the charges table and in the callout under the periods table. <https://www.vicroads.vic.gov.au/registrations/manage-registrations/concession-discounts/concession-holder-discounts>
- **Pay your registration** (VicRoads). The four ways to pay in the "How to pay" section, and the bank payment block with a biller code and a reference. <https://www.vicroads.vic.gov.au/registrations/pay-your-registration>
- **FAQs registration renewal** (VicRoads). A notice goes out about six weeks before the registration expires, a payment takes one to two days to be processed, and a renewed registration starts from the previous expiry date whether it is paid early or late. Those are the sentences in the callout on page 1 and in "Important information" on page 2. <https://www.vicroads.vic.gov.au/contact/faqs/faqs-registration>
- **Certificate of registration** (VicRoads). This page carries the official picture of a notice. It gave nine of the ten field labels in the green box on page 2, all except the expiry date. It also gave the fact that the certificate is printed on the second page of the notice. It is the only official picture of a notice that was used. <https://www.vicroads.vic.gov.au/registrations/manage-registrations/certificate-of-registration>
- **Road Safety Act 1986** (Victoria). The law that makes it an offence to use an unregistered vehicle on a highway, which is the first sentence of "Important information" on page 2. No web address for it is recorded, so it is named here without a link.

Made up, not taken from any source: the names Driver and Vehicle Registry, Wenhurst Registry Services Pty Ltd, Road Injury Compensation Fund and Kelsworth Post, the "licencedesk" badge and the colours, the customer Mrs M A Wilson and her address, and every number that identifies somebody or something: the customer number, the registration number, the engine number, the ABN, the biller code, the bank reference and all three phone numbers. The whole look of page 1 is invented, because the official picture blurs that half out: the wording, the order of the blocks and the columns of the charges table were worked out rather than copied. The registration fee of \$352.70 is nowhere published on its own; it was worked out by taking the transport accident part away from the published yearly total. Her postcode is invented, so it appears in no risk zone list, and the notice treats her as living in the metropolitan high risk zone because the persona places her in the northern suburbs of Melbourne. The 3 month and 6 month totals were worked out by scaling the year and adding the service fee: the transport accident side of that sum follows a published rule, but nothing published says how the registration side is rounded. Printing all three periods side by side was a choice, because it is not known whether a real notice prints more than the period it is for. The tare of 1265 was invented for this car and never checked. The date of 15 September 2026 in the callout was a choice as well: the published guidance says a payment takes one to two days, but it does not say that a notice prints a suggested date. The payment phone line is an ordinary Victorian landline, because the short number range a real registry uses is not available for fiction. One thing was kept real. The make of the car, Toyota, is printed as it is, because the regulation requires a certificate to show the make, and a make that does not exist would describe the world wrongly. No maker's logo, colours or contact details appear anywhere.

## The six fields

### document_type

**Vehicle registration renewal notice.** Page 1, under the address block, the large heading "VEHICLE REGISTRATION RENEWAL NOTICE". The details table under that heading ends with a row labelled "Registration expires", and the table below it is headed "What you are paying for". The answer key stores it as `vehicle_registration_renewal_notice`. This field is free text and the experiments do not score it.

### issuer

**Driver and Vehicle Registry.** Page 1, top left, the first line of the letterhead, in bold above the dark badge that reads "licencedesk". The same name is printed at the bottom of page 1, in the line "Issued by Wenhurst Registry Services Pty Ltd on behalf of Driver and Vehicle Registry", and again in the footer of page 2 beside the ABN and the website. It is the registry the car is registered with, and the notice is issued in its name.

### action_required

**Pay Driver and Vehicle Registry.** Page 1, the bordered box across the middle of the page, headed "TOTAL AMOUNT DUE" with "\$479.40" below it on the left, and headed "PAY BEFORE" with "17 September 2026" below it on the right. The table above the box is headed "What you are paying for", and lower down the page a section headed "How to pay" lists four ways to pay. The value starts with one of the contract's eight action words, and the experiments score that word.

### due_date

**2026-09-17.** Page 1, the bordered box, under "PAY BEFORE", printed as "17 September 2026". The same date is in the details table at the top of page 1, on the row "Registration expires", and on page 2 in the green certificate box under "EXPIRY DATE", where it is written as 17/09/2026. Page 1 spells the month out and page 2 writes it in numbers; the answer key writes it as year, month, day. The registration runs out on that date, and the notice asks her to pay before it.

### amount

**\$479.40.** Page 1, the bordered box, under "TOTAL AMOUNT DUE". The same figure is the last line of the charges table above the box, on the row "Total for a 12 month registration", and it is the amount on the "12 months" row of the "Other registration periods" table, which is shaded and marked "the period shown on this notice". It is what the charges table comes to: the registration fee, less the pensioner concession, plus the transport accident charge and the insurance duty.

### reference

**812 466 305.** Page 1, the details table under the heading, second row, labelled "Customer number". The same number is printed on page 2 in the green certificate box, under "CUSTOMER NO.". The "How to pay" section asks for it by name as well: the "Online" method says she will need her customer number. The contract asks for the reference, account or customer number the person must quote, and the customer number is the number this registry keeps her records under.
