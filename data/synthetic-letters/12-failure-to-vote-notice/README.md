# 12 · Failure to vote notice

Sample `SYN-0021`, printed in small grey type at the bottom of the page, right of centre, between the legal reference on the left and the form code on the right. One page. A notice dated 16 June 2026 from a fictional election office, the Ballot Administration Office, to Mrs M A Wilson, saying the records show she did not vote at the Calderfield City Council by-election on 14 March 2026 and asking her to respond. The answer key is [`ground-truth.json`](ground-truth.json).

## Where it comes from

This notice followed two official blank forms for its layout, but they are federal forms, not Victorian ones. The Australian Electoral Commission publishes its blank failure to vote notices for federal elections. The Victorian Electoral Commission does not publish a blank copy of its own notice. This notice is about a Victorian council by-election, so its rules come from Victorian law. The layout, the section headings and much of the wording were adapted from the federal forms, and the legal content was changed to the Victorian rules.

So the notice was built from Victorian law and from the two federal forms:

- **Local Government (Electoral) Regulations 2020, version 6, regulations 85 and 86** (Victorian Government, authorised version). Regulation 86(1) lists what this notice must contain: a request for a signed statement about whether she had a sufficient excuse, the time allowed, which must be at least 21 days, and what may happen if she does not give a sufficient excuse. Those three items became the sections she fills in and signs, the due date, and the third paragraph of the letter. Regulation 86(3) gave the paragraph saying another person aged 18 or over may respond for her if she is away, ill, disabled or infirm. Regulation 86(5) gave the warning about a false excuse inside the signature box. Regulation 85 requires the voters' roll to be checked after the election, which is why the letter opens with "the scrutiny of the voters' roll". The footer names regulation 86(1) as the law the notice is sent under. <https://content.legislation.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-06/20-72sra006-authorised.pdf>
- **Local Government Act 2020, version 25, sections 266 and 267** (Victorian Government, authorised version). Section 266 makes voting at a council election compulsory and sets a maximum penalty of 1 penalty unit. Section 267(4) sets the infringement penalty for not voting at half a penalty unit. Section 267(1) says the prosecution officer is the Victorian Electoral Commission or a person it appoints, which is why a separate office can send the notice and sign it "Prosecution Officer". This version was in force from 15 April 2026, before the date on the notice. <https://content.legislation.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-04/20-9aa025-authorised.pdf>
- **Monetary Units Act 2004, section 7(4)** (Victorian Government). The rule that penalty amounts are rounded to whole dollars. Half of the 2025 to 26 penalty unit of \$203.51 is \$101.755, which rounds to the \$102.00 printed on the notice.
- **Fines and reviews** (Victorian Electoral Commission). The 28 days a person is given to respond to the notice. The regulation only sets a minimum of 21 days. The page also lists \$102 as the infringement penalty for 2025 to 26, and the amount worked out from the Act was checked against it. <https://www.vec.vic.gov.au/voting/fines-and-reviews>
- **Notice: Failure to vote, form EF044, version 20250319** (Australian Electoral Commission). The overall layout of the page. It gave the single A4 page and the header in two columns, with "Address for correspondence" on the left and the office details on the right. It gave the recipient's address on the left at the same height as the title on the right, and the red "Due Date:" line directly under the title. It gave the grey body text with a near black header, address block and footer, the Act names in italics, and the small footer with the legal reference in italics on the left and the form code on the right. <https://www.aec.gov.au/about_aec/cea-notices/files/2025/Appendix-C-EF044-Failure-to-vote-notice.pdf>
- **Penalty Notice: Apparent failure to vote, form EF043-a NV1, version 20250528** (Australian Electoral Commission). The three coloured sections and the box she signs. Each section is a pale block with a dark strip across the top, in blue, purple and orange, with the colours taken from this form. "OPTION 1" and "OPTION 2" are small solid tabs at the right end of the strip. The first section, "IF YOU DID VOTE", has no option number. The centred sentence "If you did not vote, you have two options:" sits between the sections, with its first half in bold. The large tick boxes, the grey box with a white inside for the signature, phone number and date, and the dotted date line "Date:....../......../............." also come from this form. The section headings and the opening sentence "I am writing to notify you, under" follow this form's wording. The form uses two different reds, one for the due date and one for the return instruction, and so does this notice. <https://www.aec.gov.au/about_aec/cea-notices/files/2025/Appendix-A-EF043-a-NV1-Non-voter-notice-1.pdf>

Made up, not taken from any source: the office name Ballot Administration Office and its "Enforcement of Compulsory Voting" line, Calderfield City Council and its by-election, Mrs M A Wilson and her address, the elector ID, the notice number and the pattern of both numbers, the phone number, the website, the PO Box address, the barcode, which is a row of bars that encodes nothing, and the form code "BAO-NV1 20251117" at the bottom right. The header has no logo, crest or brand name. The PO Box is used instead of a reply paid number, because reply paid numbers are real identifiers issued by Australia Post. Real Victorian council elections are mostly held by post. This by-election is written as one with voting on a single Saturday. The biggest invented part is the layout of the whole notice. No blank Victorian notice is published, so the layout is borrowed from the federal forms above, and a real Victorian notice may be arranged differently or may have no coloured sections. Printing the \$102.00 figure is also a choice. The regulation asks the notice to say what action may be taken, not how much it costs, and a real Victorian notice may not print an amount. The QR code, fold lines, return address block and payment section of the federal forms were left out.

## The six fields

### document_type

**Apparent failure to vote notice.** Page 1, top right, the large title reads "Notice" and under it "Apparent failure to vote". The footer at the bottom left says "Notice under regulation 86(1) of the Local Government (Electoral) Regulations 2020". The answer key stores it as `apparent_failure_to_vote_notice`. This field is free text and the experiments do not score it.

### issuer

**Ballot Administration Office.** Page 1, top right, the bold name "Ballot Administration Office", with "Enforcement of Compulsory Voting", the phone number and the website under it. The same name is printed at the top left in the "Address for correspondence" block, under "Prosecution Officer". The letter is signed "Prosecution Officer", and the red line at the bottom asks for the notice to be returned to that address. It is the office that sends the notice and receives it back.

### action_required

**Complete and sign one of the three sections of this notice (that you did vote, that you did not vote and have a sufficient excuse, or that you did not vote and have no sufficient excuse) and return the notice to the Prosecution Officer so that it arrives by 14 July 2026.** Page 1, second paragraph of the letter: "You must complete and sign the appropriate section of this notice and return it within 28 days, so that it reaches this office by 14 July 2026." The three sections fill the lower half of the page: the blue "IF YOU DID VOTE", the purple "OPTION 1" with "IF YOU HAVE A SUFFICIENT EXCUSE FOR NOT VOTING", and the orange "OPTION 2" with "IF YOU DO NOT HAVE A SUFFICIENT EXCUSE FOR NOT VOTING". Under them is the grey box "COMPLETE THIS SECTION BEFORE RETURNING THIS NOTICE" with the signature line. The red line at the bottom says "Please return this notice to the address for correspondence shown at the top of this page." That address, at the top left, starts with "Prosecution Officer". The sentence "If you did not vote, you have two options:" and the line "DO NOT COMPLETE OPTION 2 IF YOU COMPLETE THIS PART." show that she fills in only one section. This field is free text and the experiments do not score it.

### due_date

**2026-07-14.** Page 1, top right, directly under the title, in red bold type: "Due Date: 14 July 2026". The same date is in the second paragraph of the letter ("so that it reaches this office by 14 July 2026") and at the start of the third paragraph ("If you do not provide a sufficient excuse by 14 July 2026"). It is the date the completed notice must reach the office. The letter is dated 16 June 2026 and gives 28 days to respond, which ends on the same day. The page writes the month as a word. The answer key writes the date as year, month, day.

### amount

**No payment required.** Page 1, the orange "OPTION 2" section near the bottom, last line: "DO NOT SEND PAYMENT WITH THIS NOTICE." The page has no amount due line, no payment methods and no payment slip. The notice asks for a signed statement, not for money. The letter mentions \$102.00 in the third paragraph and in Option 2 as the penalty on an infringement notice that would be sent later. Neither sentence asks her to pay anything with this notice.

### reference

**NV2026-732810.** Page 1, top right, the last line of the title block, under the red due date: "Notice number NV2026-732810". It is printed only once on the page. The contract asks for the reference, account or customer number the person must quote. The notice number identifies this notice, and this notice is what she has to complete and return.
