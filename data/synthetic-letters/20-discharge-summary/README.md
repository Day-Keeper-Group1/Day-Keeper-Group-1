# 20 · Discharge summary

Sample `SYN-0013`, printed in small type at the bottom right of each page. Three pages. A hospital discharge summary from a fictional hospital, Bramworth Hospital, written to Mrs Margaret Anne Wilson's general practitioner after a five day stay in May 2026 for a broken wrist, with a copy given to her when she went home. The answer key is [`ground-truth.json`](ground-truth.json).

## Where it comes from

This discharge summary did not follow an official sample or an annotated example of a whole discharge summary. It was built up from a national guideline, without one.

No government body publishes a blank or a completed discharge summary that can be copied. The national guideline covers how a discharge summary should look on a screen, so there is no official printed version to follow either. The rules the synthetic data was made under do not allow a real hospital's discharge summary to be used as a template.

So the document was built up from what the national guideline says a discharge summary must contain, from the two small example tables printed inside that guideline, and from Victorian health department policy:

- **National guidelines for presentation of electronic discharge summaries** (Australian Commission on Safety and Quality in Health Care, 2025). This is the only guideline the document follows. It gave the order of the sections, what each section must contain, the columns and the column headings of each table, and the four values the "Status" column of the medicines table is allowed to take. It also gave three rules about writing things down plainly: a patient with no known allergies must be recorded as having none, a medicine list with nothing stopped must say so, and a result still pending at discharge must be marked as pending and repeated under "Recommendations". It is also the guideline that says the Procedures section is removed from the document altogether when there was no procedure. That deletion rule is written for the Procedures section only. Two tables inside the guideline are printed as worked examples, and they gave the look of the tables here: a light grey heading row, black bold heading text, thin lines around every cell, and plain white rows underneath. They also gave the habit of bolding only the medicine name and its strength in the first column of the medicines table, leaving the form and the route unbolded. <https://www.safetyandquality.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-11/national-guidelines-for-presentation-of-electronic-discharge-summaries.pdf>
- **Managing referrals to non-admitted specialist services** (Department of Health, Victoria). Section 9 says the hospital must send a written handover to the referring clinician, to the patient and to the patient's general practitioner within five working days of discharge. That is why the "Recipients" table on page 3 exists, and why it names the general practitioner as the primary recipient with the patient holding a copy. <https://www.health.vic.gov.au/publications/specialist-clinics-in-victorian-public-hospitals-access-policy>
- **New appointment patient letter template** (Department of Health, Victoria). Its layout was not used here. It is recorded because the first row of the "Follow-up appointments" table is the appointment the hospital will write to her about later, and the specialist clinics telephone number printed in that row is the same number printed on the outpatient appointment letter elsewhere in this set. <https://www.health.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-12/New-appointment-patient-letter-template.docx>

No law is quoted anywhere on the three pages. The standing wording on a discharge summary is about clinical safety, not about legal rights, so there is nothing to cite.

Made up, not taken from any source: the hospital Bramworth Hospital, the health service Thornhurst Health and the Marnworth Medical Clinic, the patient Mrs Margaret Anne Wilson with her address and date of birth, and the four people named on the pages, Dr Anh Nguyen, Mr Andrew Clarke, Dr Susan Patel and Ms Michelle Taylor. Common Australian surnames were chosen on purpose, because a rare name can point at one real person. Writing "Mr" rather than "Dr" in front of a surgeon's name is the real Australian habit, not a mistake. Every number on the pages was generated: the seven digit medical record number, the two sixteen digit healthcare identifiers, which are printed as one run of digits with no spaces, and the seven telephone numbers, which serve seven different purposes: her own telephone, the hospital switchboard, the ward, the author of the summary, the specialist clinics, the general practice, and the community rehabilitation service. All of the clinical content was written: the diagnoses, the six medicines with their doses and directions, the ceased medicine, the alerts, the five investigation results and every sentence of the clinical summary. The medicine names and doses are ordinary Australian generic names at usual strengths, but no part of it has been checked against clinical guidance or by a clinician. There was no operation in the story, so the document has no "Procedures" section at all, while the "Allergies and adverse reactions" section stays and says "No known allergies"; the guideline treats those two sections in opposite ways, and this document shows both rules at once. The dates of the stay were chosen, and the discharge date, 17/05/2026, falls on a Sunday, which happens in Australian hospitals but is a little unusual for a patient who lives alone; it was left as it is. The physiotherapy appointment on Wednesday 27 May 2026 was placed on the tenth day after discharge to leave time for the referral. Listing only five investigation results was a choice to hold the document to three pages. The recommendation about home support names no service and no organisation, so the words "a home support assessment" and "the assessment service" stand in for names. The two headings on the "Recommendations" table are the two short ones the example table prints, "Recommendations" and "For action by", and the rough timing for each item sits inside the wording of the row instead. Everything about the paper version was our choice: repeating the patient block and the hospital block at the top of every page, where the three pages break, the footer line, the page numbering, and where the sample number sits. The exact greys, type sizes, line spacing and column widths in millimetres were set by us, because the guideline describes a screen and not a printed page.

## The six fields

### document_type

**Discharge summary.** Every page prints the large heading "Discharge Summary" under the two header blocks, with "Page 1 of 3", "Page 2 of 3" and "Page 3 of 3" on the right of that heading. Page 1 has a "Presentation details" table with a "Discharge date" column and a "Discharge destination" column. The answer key stores it as `discharge_summary`. This field is free text and the experiments do not score it.

### issuer

**Bramworth Hospital.** Page 1, top right, the name in large bold type under the small caption "HOSPITAL DETAILS", with the address "120 Fernlea Street, CALDERFIELD VIC 3999" and two telephone numbers under it. The same block is repeated at the top of pages 2 and 3. The footer of every page reads "Prepared by Dr Anh Nguyen, Orthopaedic Registrar, Bramworth Hospital." Page 3 names it again in the "Follow-up appointments" table, as "Bramworth Hospital Specialist Clinics". It is the name the document is sent under, printed as the heading of the sender's block. "Thornhurst Health" appears in the same block as a value beside the small label "Health service".

### action_required

**Attend general practitioner.** Page 3, the "Follow-up appointments" table, second row. The row reads "General practitioner review of medicines and blood pressure" under "Appointment", "Within 7 days of discharge" under "When", "To be arranged by the patient" under "Booking status", and "Dr Susan Patel, Marnworth Medical Clinic" with a telephone number under "Provider and contact". It is the only one of the three rows whose booking status is "To be arranged by the patient"; the first row is to be arranged by the hospital and the third row is already booked. The row is an appointment for her to attend with her own general practitioner. The value starts with one of the contract's eight action words, and the experiments score that word.

### due_date

**Not applicable.** No date is printed for the general practitioner review. Page 3, the "Follow-up appointments" table, second row, the "When" column says "Within 7 days of discharge", which is a length of time and not a date. Page 1, the "Presentation details" table, prints "17/05/2026" under "Discharge date", written day first in the Australian way. A date for the review would have to be worked out by adding the seven days to that discharge date, and the answer key records only what the page prints, so it has `null` for this field.

### amount

**No payment required.** No amount of money is printed anywhere on the three pages. There is no payment section, no total and no account number to pay against. The document is a clinical handover from the hospital to her general practitioner, and the "Information provided to the patient" list on page 3 says she was given "Two weeks supply of the medicines listed above", with no charge printed beside it. The answer key has `null` for this field.

### reference

**6938509.** Page 1, top left, the patient block, the label "Medical Record Number (UR)" in the label column with "6938509" in the value column beside it. The same block with the same number is repeated at the top of pages 2 and 3. The label is printed separately from the number, so the answer is the digits on their own. The contract asks for the reference, account or customer number the person must quote, and the medical record number is the number this hospital keeps her record under.
