# 19 · Outpatient appointment letter

Sample `SYN-0012`, printed in small type at the bottom right of each page. Two pages. A letter from a fictional public health service, Thornhurst Health, to Mrs M A Wilson, telling her she has an appointment at the Orthopaedic Clinic on 23 June 2026, with a map of the hospital on the back. The answer key is [`ground-truth.json`](ground-truth.json).

## Where it comes from

This letter did follow an official sample document. The Victorian Department of Health publishes a blank Word template for exactly this letter, with the placeholders still in it, and this letter copies that template word for word. The wording of every sentence, the order of the blocks, the four rows of the appointment table and the six items in the bring list are the template's own. The template was also unpacked to read its font sizes, its bold, its column widths and its shading, so the look of the page follows the real file rather than a guess.

- **New appointment patient letter template** (Department of Health, Victoria, December 2022). The one source for the layout and the wording. It gave the box for the barcode and the UR number, the contact block in the letterhead, the "Need an Interpreter?" lines, the salutation, the sentence "You have a new appointment with the Thornhurst Health Specialist Clinics.", the six items under "Please bring with you:", the four rows of the "New Appointment" table, the sentence about calling to cancel or change, the closing sentence and the signature block. <https://www.health.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-12/New-appointment-patient-letter-template.docx>
- **Specialist clinics communication toolkit guide** (Department of Health, Victoria, January 2023). The department's own rules for writing to patients: keep the letter under one page, use a plain font, use bold for emphasis, use a table for the appointment details, and put an easy to follow map on the back. It is the only reason page 2 is a map. It also says a reminder letter goes out four weeks before the appointment, which is why the letter is dated 28 days before the appointment date. <https://www.health.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-01/specialist-clinics-communication-toolkit-guide-improving-written-communication-to-patients-referrers-09012023.docx>
- **About new appointments information sheet** (Department of Health, Victoria, December 2022). This sheet was not rendered as part of the sample. It was read to confirm where three things live. The sentence saying patients with a current Medicare card do not need to pay is on this sheet and not in the letter, so the letter carries no amount and no currency symbol. The rule about missing two appointments is also on this sheet and not in the letter. It also confirmed the wording used across the state for telling the clinic you cannot come, which is "as soon as possible" and names no number of hours. That is why the line in this letter about cancelling or changing the appointment does not give a notice period either. <https://www.health.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-12/About-new-appointments-information-sheet.docx>
- **Managing referrals to non-admitted specialist services in Victorian public health services** (Department of Health, Victoria). The policy that says what an appointment letter must contain: the date and time, what to bring and how to prepare, where the service physically is including a map or written directions, and what the patient should do if they cannot come. Those are the four blocks of this letter. The letter does not quote the policy. <https://www.health.vic.gov.au/publications/specialist-clinics-in-victorian-public-hospitals-access-policy>
- **Patient has multiple appointments patient letter template** (Department of Health, Victoria, December 2022). Read as a comparison and not followed. A series of appointments is a different template with a different opening sentence and a wider table, so this letter keeps one appointment and one four row table. <https://www.health.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-12/Patient-has-multiple-appointments-patient-letter-template.docx>
- **Patient did not attend patient letter template** (Department of Health, Victoria, December 2022). Read as a comparison and not followed. That letter carries a missed appointment heading and a date in the past. This is a first appointment, so no past date is printed anywhere on the page. <https://www.health.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-12/Patient-did-not-attend-patient-letter-template.docx>
- **Health service has changed appointment date or time patient letter template** (Department of Health, Victoria, December 2022). Read as a comparison and not followed. In that letter the bring list sits after the table. It showed that the position of the list moves between templates, so this letter keeps the list before the table, as the new appointment template has it. <https://www.health.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-12/Health-service-has-changed-appointment-date-or-time-patient-letter-template.docx>
- **Referral accepted but no appointment has yet been made patient letter template** (Department of Health, Victoria, February 2023). Read as a comparison and not followed. It is the template that carries a Clinic, Referral date and Referrer block. That block belongs to this one and not to the new appointment letter, so it is not on the page. <https://www.health.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-02/Referral-accepted-but-no-appointment-has-yet-been-made-patient-letter-template.docx>
- **Patient to contact health service to arrange appointment patient letter template** (Department of Health, Victoria, December 2022). Read as a comparison and not followed. That letter asks the patient to phone within two weeks, which is a deadline rather than an appointment time. This sample deliberately carries only the appointment time. <https://www.health.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-12/Patient-to-contact-health-service-to-arrange-appointment-patient-letter-template.docx>
- **Confirmation of discharge after last appointment patient letter template** (Department of Health, Victoria, December 2022). Read as a comparison and not followed. It has almost the same layout with the opposite meaning. It is recorded so that a future letter of that kind uses the same letterhead and signature block as this one. <https://www.health.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-12/Confirmation-of-discharge-after-last-appointment-patient-letter-template.docx>
- **Patient discharged as has cancelled many appointments, new referral needed, patient letter template** (Department of Health, Victoria, December 2022). Read as a comparison and not followed. It asks the patient to go back to a general practitioner for a new referral, which is a third kind of action in the same family of letters. <https://www.health.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-12/Patient-discharged-as-has-cancelled-many-appointments-new-referral-needed-patient-letter-template.docx>
- **About wait lists information sheet** (Department of Health, Victoria, December 2022). Read as a comparison and not followed. It was archived to complete the set of official documents in this family. <https://www.health.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-12/About-wait-lists-information-sheet.docx>
- **Health Insurance Regulations 2018** (Commonwealth, Federal Register of Legislation). They set how long a referral to a specialist stays valid. A real appointment letter does not mention this, and neither does this one. <https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2018L01365/latest/text>

Made up, not taken from any source: the health service name Thornhurst Health and the hospital name Bramworth Hospital, the patient Mrs M A Wilson and her address, the manager who signs the letter, Julie Thompson, and every number and address on the page, meaning the UR number, the three phone numbers, the fax number, the web address and the email address. The three phone numbers are the one in the letterhead, the one to call to cancel or change the appointment, and the interpreter line. The street names 120 Fernlea Street, Marlow Street and Denby Road are invented, and so is the suburb the hospital sits in. The floor and building, "Ground Floor, Building C", were kept descriptive on purpose so that no invented building name is needed. The UR number is seven digits because no published rule says how long a UR number is. The barcode above it only looks like a barcode; it encodes nothing and cannot be scanned. The logo shape and its dark red are invented, and apart from the logo, page 1 is black and grey, as the official template is. The map on page 2 uses that same dark red for its six numbered markers and for Building C. The four small icons beside the table rows and beside the interpreter line were drawn to match the meaning of the template's own icons rather than copied. The letterhead phone number and the clinic phone number were made different numbers, which the template does not settle either way. The way the letter date is written, and the office hours in the sentence about calling the clinic, were chosen because the template leaves both as placeholders. The whole of page 2 is invented: the official template says nothing about the back of the letter beyond the words "turn over to see map", so the streets, the buildings, the numbered legend, the north arrow and the scale bar are all our own. Page 2 was built so that it carries no date, no phone number and no instruction. Page 2 also does not repeat the letterhead from page 1. The official template is a single page Word document, so the back of the letter is not part of the official file at all. Leaving the letterhead off the back page is a normal printing habit, but no official rule asks for it, so that was our own choice. Four things on page 1 that look like our choices are not: the interpreter lines have no box and no shading, the letter date is left aligned, the body text is 12 point, and there is no Clinic, Referral date and Referrer block. Each of those was checked inside the official template file and follows it.

## The six fields

### document_type

**Outpatient appointment letter.** Page 1, the bold heading above the shaded table reads "New Appointment". The sentence above the bring list reads "You have a new appointment with the Thornhurst Health Specialist Clinics." The first row of the table is "Clinic" with "Orthopaedic Clinic" beside it. The answer key stores it as `outpatient_appointment_letter`. This field is free text and the experiments do not score it.

### issuer

**Thornhurst Health.** Page 1, top left, the logo sits beside the name "Thornhurst Health" with "SPECIALIST CLINICS" in smaller letters under it. The contact block at the top right is headed "Thornhurst Health Specialist Clinics", and the body of the letter says "You have a new appointment with the Thornhurst Health Specialist Clinics." That is the health service sending the letter. The address row of the table and the signature block name "Bramworth Hospital Specialist Clinics", which is the campus the clinic sits on. Scoring accepts "Thornhurst Health Specialist Clinics" as well, because one name contains the other.

### action_required

**Attend Orthopaedic Clinic.** Page 1, the shaded "New Appointment" table, first row, "Clinic" with "Orthopaedic Clinic" beside it, and the sentence above the bring list, "You have a new appointment with the Thornhurst Health Specialist Clinics." The letter exists to tell her to come to that clinic at the time in the table. The six items under "Please bring with you:" are things to bring to the same appointment, not separate requests. The value starts with one of the contract's eight action words, and the experiments score that word.

### due_date

**2026-06-23.** Page 1, the "New Appointment" table, the "Date" row, which reads "Tuesday 23 Jun 2026" in bold. The row below it, labelled "Time", reads "10:45AM". The date in that row is the day the appointment happens, and it is the only date the letter asks her to act on. The page writes the weekday first, then the day, then a short month, then the year; the answer key writes it as year, month, day.

### amount

**No payment required.** There is no amount and no currency symbol anywhere on either page. The letter never uses the word pay. The only money related words on page 1 are in the bring list, "Your Medicare Card", which is a card to bring rather than a payment to make. The letter asks for nothing to be paid, so there is no amount. The answer key has `null` for this field.

### reference

**UR 6938509.** Page 1, top right, inside the bordered box under the contact block: a barcode with one line printed under it in a typewriter face, "UR 6938509". There is no colon after "UR", the whole line is set in the same face and size, and nothing else on either page labels this number. The number does not appear again on either page. It is the only reference number on the letter, and the contract asks for the reference the person must quote, so the key is that line as it is printed, with "UR" and the spacing the page shows.

### identifiers

Every number the letter prints that identifies her, something she holds, or this matter, each under the label printed beside it, written as printed. The first list is the numbers a reading must find; the reference is one of them, and a reading that gives any of them as the reference is right. The experiments score whether every number in the first list is found.
