Where a model is called, and where it is not

Two places in the whole product call a model, and everything else is ordinary code and ordinary SQL. The call that looks at photographs is the expensive one, so it does all of the looking in a single pass: it divides the pile into letters and reads their fields at the same time.

Call 1 The reading
Runs once per upload, on the whole pile.
In
every photograph in the batch, as images
Up to ten in one upload, in whatever order she happened to take them.
→
It does two things in one pass
  • divides the pile into letters
    a manifest: which photographs make each letter, in reading order, what it appears to be, why they are one letter, and that letter's full text.
  • reads the six fields of each letter
    document_type · issuer · action_required · due_date · amount · reference, plus an optional due_time.
→
Out
a manifest, plus six fields per letter, each with a status
Confirmed, uncertain, or unreadable. A photograph that is no letter at all is named as one, and becomes nothing.
vision tokens, the expensive one the photographs are looked at once and once only

Why the two jobs are one call. The evidence for a page boundary is visual: a letterhead, a logo, a change of layout. Almost none of it survives being flattened into text. Asking the reader for the words and then paying a second model to reconstruct the boundaries is discarding the evidence and then buying a guess.

Call 2 The matcher
Runs once per letter, when it might be the rest of something she already has.
In
the new letter's text, plus three tools over a view of her archive
The view is assembled from the database on every read, so it is never a copy and cannot go stale.
→
It searches, and code does not narrow first
  • glob(pattern)   grep(pattern, path_glob?)   read(path)
    The model is handed tools and a question, not a result set. It decides what to look for, how many times, and what the answers mean.
  • every pattern and hit is logged
    an accuracy record a person can read, rather than a similarity score nobody can argue with.
→
Out
a document id, or nothing, which is an answer and not a failure to answer
Nothing means the letter is new. Attaching pages to the wrong letter is the mistake worth avoiding.
text only, cheap, a few turns

Why not a query written by us in advance. Letters are not a genre. A party invitation has no issuer worth matching on and no reference number, and the save the date it belongs with names a suburb where the invitation names a street. A query written in advance has to anticipate the shape of somebody's correspondence, and there is no shape.

Not a model call these look like judgement, and every one of them is ordinary code
Whether what she must do has changed
→
!= on four values: action_required, due_date, due_time, amount
Whose archive gets searched
→
a WHERE user_id = ... clause the model cannot set
Whether a manifest contradicts itself
→
arithmetic on page numbers: a photograph cited twice, a photograph never mentioned, a page number past the end
Whether a task is overdue
→
its due date compared with today, in her timezone, worked out while drawing the screen
Whether a reminder fires
→
the dispatcher reads the task at the moment the clock rings

Each of these is a place where being merely probably right was not good enough, so it was moved out of the model's hands and into something that can be tested.

What this costs to run
  • One upload of ten photographs is one vision call, whatever it turns out to contain.
  • One matcher call per letter, over text.
  • Everything else in the product is ordinary code and ordinary SQL.
No model access is needed to run the product end to end. A mock reader that hedges and fails on purpose is the default, so the whole flow can be built and demonstrated before a key exists.
