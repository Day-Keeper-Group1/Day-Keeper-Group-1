The ten tables, and which step writes each

A letter moves through the steps of the previous part, and each one writes a different part of the database. Nothing is written twice, and nothing that can be worked out at the moment it is asked for is stored. The definition of every column, with the reason it exists, is in db/schema.sql.

Step 1
Photograph
The pages arrive. Nothing has looked at them yet.
documents
status, uploaded_at, issuer, document_type
One row per letter, created at processing before anything is known about it.
→ users
document_pages
page_number, storage_key, mime_type
One row per photograph. The bytes live in the object store; this row is how to find them again.
→ documents
Step 2
Read
The one model call, and the record of what it was asked and what it said.
extraction_runs
status, model, started_at, finished_at
One reading per letter. There is no second attempt, so there is no attempt number.
→ documents
extracted_fields
field_key, value, status
Six rows, sometimes more. A field the model could not read is a row saying so, not a missing row.
→ extraction_runs
ai_prompt_logs
prompt, response, tokens
What was sent and what came back, so an answer can be argued with months later.
→ extraction_runs
Step 3
Check
She looks at the reading and decides whether it is her letter.
This step writes nothing at all.

The screen shows what was read and takes no answer except yes, so there is nothing for it to record. A step that writes nothing is worth a column of its own here, because every design that asked her a question needed somewhere to put the answer.
Step 4
Confirm
Her yes is the only thing that turns a reading into something that will act.
tasks
title, due_date, due_time, state
The tick is a task's only state. Whether it is overdue is worked out from the clock, never stored.
→ users, documents
reminders
scheduled_for, channel, status
Three rows, seven, three and one day before. Written now because each one has its own fate to record.
→ tasks
Step 5
Remind, and tick
A reminder rings and reads the tick. Both write, and neither writes the other.
reminders
status → sent, skipped, failed
Written a second time, and only this column. Still open means send it; already ticked means send nothing and say so.
tasks
state, completed_at
Ticking writes here and nowhere else. No reminder is cancelled, because none needed to be.
Three tables belong to no step

users and sessions are there before a letter is, and audit_logs records what was done to an account rather than what a letter said. The audit table has nowhere to put the contents of a letter, and that is deliberate rather than an omission.

What is not here

There is no table for a batch of mixed letters, none for deciding which photographs make a letter, and none for joining a later upload to an earlier one. Those were the expensive parts of a larger design. The rows above are what is left when an upload is one letter by construction.
