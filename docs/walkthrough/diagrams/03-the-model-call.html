Where the model is called, and where it is not

One place in the whole product calls a model, and everything after it is ordinary code and ordinary SQL. The interesting engineering is concentrated in a single request, and the rest of the system can be read without thinking about a model at all.

The reading Runs once per upload, on the pages of one letter.
In
every photograph in the upload, as images
All of them are pages of one letter. Which letter a page belongs to was settled by the person who photographed it, not inferred here.

What it does
Looks at the pages and answers in a fixed shape
  • document_type · issuer · action_required
    what the letter is, who sent it, and the one thing it asks of her.
  • due_date · amount · reference
    the three that turn a letter into something a calendar and a bank can act on, plus an optional due_time when the page prints one.

Why the model is given the photographs. A letter is designed for a human eye, and half of what it means is carried by arrangement rather than by words: a box drawn around the number that matters, a heading in bold, and five payment methods printed side by side, each with its own reference number and nothing but a column to say which belongs to which. A model that looks at the page has all of that in front of it, and answers in one pass with the arrangement still intact.
