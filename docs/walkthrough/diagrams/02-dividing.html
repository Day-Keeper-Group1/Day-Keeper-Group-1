How nine photographs become six letters

The pile has no order and no grouping, and nothing about the act of photographing says which sheet belongs with which. So the pass that looks at the pictures is asked to say. It hands back a manifest: the letters it found, the photographs that make up each one, why those photographs are one letter, and which photographs are not letters at all.

The reading
One model call. All nine photographs go in together, because the evidence for a page boundary is visual: a letterhead, the same logo as the sheet before, a different layout.

Four things ride on this answer
1The page order is the reading's, not the camera's, because the reading can see “Page 2 of 3” printed on a sheet.
2Every letter is named at birth, off the letterhead, so a queue never shows three rows all saying “reading...”.
3The reason is required, because nobody will ever confirm this grouping and that sentence is its only account of itself.
4The words are transcribed per letter, so the photographs are looked at once and once only.
